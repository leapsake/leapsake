# Leapsake v0.1 — the road to shipped apps

> **This is the table of contents for everything that must happen before v0.1, in order.**
> It holds the decisions and the sequence; the work itself lives in the numbered docs below,
> each of which is **deleted the day its work lands**. If you want to know *what to pick up
> next*, this file is the only place that answers it.

**v0.1 is:** signed, installable **mobile** apps a stranger can get from both stores, with a
verified backup story and an account system that cannot strand anyone. *(Desktop was in this
sentence until 2026-08-26; it ships in a later release.)* It is **almost** not feature work —
the product is built, with two deliberate exceptions (08 and 09, below). The posture this must
not break is [`../AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) → *Product posture*.

- ✅ **09, mobile global navigation — done, 2026-08-17.** The **second and last** feature-work

  tab bar is what they show** — reshuffling tabs once the Play 14-day clock is running is the one

- ✅ **Developer accounts — done, 2026-08-19.** Both enrolled (Apple Developer Program, Google
  Play). Apple no longer blocks 07B. What is left of the latency is one clock, below.

## The one long clock left

Most of this is work you control. Two items were calendar time you cannot compress, and they
inverted the obvious ordering. **One is now closed:**

- ✅ **Apple Developer Program enrollment** — enrolled *(owner, 2026-08-19)*. Individual, $99/yr;
  the legal name is the public seller name. iOS submission (07B) is unblocked, and TestFlight is
  live: `0.1.0-alpha.1` reached it 2026-08-25 by hand, `0.1.0-alpha.2` on 2026-08-26 from
  `pnpm release`.
- ⏳ **Google Play production access** — the account exists *(owner, 2026-08-19)*, and the wall it
  guards is untouched: a closed test with **≥12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days**, which
  only starts *once a build is uploaded*. **This is still the critical path, but which wall it is
  is now open** — the rule binds *personal* accounts, and the owner is leaning toward forming the
  legal entity before stable v0.1, which replaces it with a D-U-N-S wait of up to 30 days. Either
  way the latency is calendar time that no engineering shortens; see *Open decisions* 4, and note
  its warning that **uploading from the personal account forecloses the choice**.

⚠️ **Reserve only what you will ship — and the Apple half is now committed.** An App Store Connect
record for "Leapsake" exists *(owner, 2026-08-19)*: the form only, no build, nothing shared.
Apple's transfer criteria require an app to have at least one released version to be transferable,
so this record cannot move to an org account until it ships — freeing the name and re-reserving it
is the only alternative, with a race window in between. Two things follow, both cheap now and
expensive later:

- ✅ **The record's bundle ID is `com.leapsake.app`** *(owner, 2026-08-19)*, matching
  `apps/mobile/app.json`. It is chosen at record creation and cannot be edited afterwards, so the
  iOS identity is now settled. See [04](./v0-1_04_mobile-pipeline.md).
- ⏳ **The Play-side name is not confirmed reserved** — and, per *Open decisions* 4, **should
  probably stay that way for now.** Creating the record is what claims the name, but it claims it
  *for whichever account creates it*, so doing it from the personal account while an org account
  is under consideration buys a reservation at the cost of a later transfer. Names are
  first-come-first-served per store, so this is a real if small risk to carry deliberately.

**08 and 09 both sat in front of 04 on purpose** (both now done, ✅ above), for the same reason
from two directions: 04 is where the first store build and the first store listing are cut, so
anything that changes what the app *is* (08's two OS permissions) or what it *looks like* (09's
tab bar, in every screenshot) is cheaper before that than after it. Landing either after 04 would
have meant a second store-metadata pass in the middle of the Play clock.

Mobile   ✅08 ─ ✅09 ─ ✅04-iOS ─ 04-Android ─ 14-day closed test ─┴─ 06 ─ 07

## Open decisions for owner sign-off

1. **How much of the E2E catalog gates v0.1, really?** *(smaller than it was)*
   [`v0-1_06_e2e-and-release-gate.md`](./v0-1_06_e2e-and-release-gate.md) → *The release-gate
   policy* requires the full catalog green on every platform **being released**. With desktop
   deferred, that is iOS + Android only, and the Playwright/Electron harness leaves v0.1 with
   it. What remains to decide is the mobile bar: (a) the full catalog under Maestro on both
   platforms; (b) the existing driver self-test plus a smoke subset, with the full catalog a
   fast-follow. **Leaning (b)** — still a deliberate amendment to a written gate, so it needs
   your call rather than a quiet reinterpretation. Decide before 06.
2. **Flows 6/7a in the v0.1 gate or fast-follow?** Follows from (1), but **Flow 6 largely
   answers itself**: *enable sync and pair a second device* exercises relay sync, and v0.1
   ships with `multiDevice` **off** in [`@leapsake/flags`](../packages/flags/README.md) — every
   door to it is shut. A v0.1 user cannot reach that flow, so gating the release on it would
   mean gating on a path the build does not expose, provable only by flipping a flag that
   production does not set. It belongs to the release that turns sync **on**, and until then
   the on-state's coverage is that README's obligation rather than this gate's. Flow **7a**
   (cross-device recovery) is a different case: the feature *is* reachable in v0.1, it is
   simply expensive to automate — two instances — and what it proves overlaps decision 5
   below. Decide 7a with 5, not with 6. See 06.
3. ✅ **Retired 2026-08-26** — *auto-update before or after going public* was a desktop
   question, and desktop left v0.1. It returns with
   [`desktop-packaging.md`](./desktop-packaging.md) → C.
4. **Which Play account publishes v0.1 — and therefore whether the tester wall applies at
   all?** *(reframed 2026-08-26)*

   The 12-testers-for-14-continuous-days wall is **not a law of Play**; it is a rule for
   **personal accounts created after 2023-11-13**, which ours is (2026-08-19). It has no
   documented waiver — testers who lapse do not count, re-opting restarts the clock, and
   production review takes up to a further seven days. **Organization accounts are not subject
   to it** and publish straight to production. *(Google's page defines the rule's scope as
   personal accounts and simply does not discuss orgs; the exemption is consistent across
   secondary sources but is not stated by Google in those words — confirm before relying on
   it.)* Reportedly, since 2026 Google also checks testers genuinely **used** the app, so a
   list of names who install and forget may not clear it either.

   **Owner leaning, 2026-08-26: form the legal entity before the stable v0.1 release**, which
   makes this an org publication and the wall moot. That agrees with the *eventual org move —
   yes, early* row above, and **now is the smallest that move will ever be**: nothing is
   published on Play, so there is nothing to transfer and no key custody to break.

   What it trades, rather than removes:

   - **A D-U-N-S number takes up to 30 days** and an entity must exist to get one — so the
     long pole moves from *recruiting humans* to *paperwork*, and is not obviously shorter.
     It replaces a task that can fail (people lapse) with one that merely waits.
   - **The stores' account types diverge.** Apple is already an individual record that cannot
     transfer until it ships at least one version. Survivable, but it means the org move is
     half-done for a while rather than clean.

   ⚠️ **The sequencing hazard, which is the actionable part:** a package name is claimed on
   Play by the account that first uploads it, so **uploading `com.leapsake.app` from the
   personal account to get an alpha out would claim it there**, and moving it later means
   Google's app-transfer process rather than a fresh start. The same applies to the Play-side
   *name*, still unreserved. So if the org is coming, **do not create the Play Console record
   or upload anything from the personal account** — build the `android` target and test it
   locally instead. This does not block 04; it blocks the *upload*, which is the last step.

   **Still yours to decide:** whether the entity lands before v0.1 (leaning yes) or v0.1 ships
   personal-account-first with the wall. If the latter, the 12-tester list is back and should
   start immediately, since it is then the only irreducible calendar latency left.
5. **Does v0.1 ship without merge by recovery phrase?** Deferred while building the merge
   *(owner, 2026-08-08)*. `recoverAccount` refuses a device that already holds an account exactly
   as `joinAccount` does, so merging by phrase needs the same copy-first treatment and is a second
   full flow on both clients. The gap: a user who has their account's **phrase** but not its
   password must recover on their *other* device first — which is fine unless that device is the
   one they lost. **Leaning ship-without** (password-plus-a-second-device covers the realistic
   case, and the flow it would duplicate is the most delicate one we have), but it is a real hole
   in the "cannot strand anyone" promise, so it is your call. Context:
   [`encryption/model.md`](./encryption/model.md) §7.2.2.

## Explicitly not v0.1

Everything else lives in [`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md) — including work that is *ready* and merely
non-blocking (vCard export, reminder search, dedup quality, the onboarding import nudge). The
rule is strict on purpose: **if it does not gate v0.1, it waits.** That is what keeps this list
short enough to finish.

**08 and 09 are the exceptions, and both are written down as ones** so neither can be cited as
precedent.

**08** because a reminders app with no way to reach a user outside the app is not shippable, and
email — the obvious alternative channel — is itself out of scope for v0.1. Notification *privacy
levels* and the *desktop* applier both stayed behind the rule and remain deferred to
[`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md).

**09** because the tab bar is in every store screenshot and 04 cuts the first ones. Note what it
did *not* do, which is what keeps it an exception rather than a crack: it changed navigation and
surface colour and stopped there. Typography, an icon set, and the illustrated empty states are
untouched and remain [`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md)'s *styling / the design system* — a screenshot is
cheap to re-cut for a nicer typeface, and expensive to re-cut for a different set of tabs.

**Multi-device sync is built and held back** *(owner, 2026-08-21)*, not unbuilt. The relay, the
join, the merge, and the scheduler all work; v0.1 simply does not show them, behind
`multiDevice` in [`@leapsake/flags`](../packages/flags/README.md). This is the one entry here
that is a *switch* rather than a scope line, so it carries a switch's obligations: the gates are
listed in that README, the on-state stays under test, and the removal is a v0.2 item rather than
a thing to remember. Turning it back on for a session is
`LEAPSAKE_FLAGS=multiDevice pnpm desktop`.

**Out of scope entirely for v0.1:** Windows and Linux desktop, matching the E2E host policy —
blocked, not waived. Windows code-signing certs carry their own procurement latency and, for
OV/EV, hardware-token logistics.
