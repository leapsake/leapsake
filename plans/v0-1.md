# Leapsake v0.1 — the road to shipped apps

> **This is the table of contents for everything that must happen before v0.1, in order.**
> It holds the decisions and the sequence; the work itself lives in the numbered docs below,
> each of which is **deleted the day its work lands**. If you want to know *what to pick up
> next*, this file is the only place that answers it.

**v0.1 is:** a signed, installable **iOS** app a stranger can get from the App Store, with a
verified backup story — **an export, since 2026-09-06** ([`ios-ga.md`](./ios-ga.md) → 1), this
clause having outlived the plan that dropped it — and an account system that cannot strand
anyone. *(Desktop left this
sentence on 2026-08-26 and **Android on 2026-09-06** — both ship in later releases, and the
second of those is an account decision rather than an engineering one: see* The account
sequence *below.)* It is **almost** not feature work —
the product is built, with two deliberate exceptions (08 and 09, below). The posture this must
not break is [`../AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md) → *Product posture*.

## The account sequence *(settled 2026-09-06)*

**There is no long clock left in v0.1.** The two items that were calendar time nobody could
compress have both left it — Apple's enrollment by completing, Play's tester wall by moving to
a release that is not this one. What replaces them is an ordering, and the ordering is forced
at one end and free at the other.

**The sequence, in full:**

1. **v0.1 ships iOS only, from the personal account.** GA in the App Store.
2. **Leapsake incorporates**, and gets a D-U-N-S number (up to 30 days, and the entity must
   exist first — so this paperwork **starts now**, in parallel, not after step 1).
3. **The iOS record transfers to the company.**
4. **Android starts under the company account** — [`android-pipeline.md`](./android-pipeline.md).
   Its first Play upload is its only one, and it claims `com.leapsake.app` correctly.
5. **macOS follows, signed under the company's Developer ID from its first release** —
   [`desktop-packaging.md`](./desktop-packaging.md), and see the key-custody note below for why
   that ordering is not incidental.

**Step 1 before step 3 is forced by Apple.** Transfer criteria require an app to have at least
one **released** version, so the record cannot move to a company account until it ships. Freeing
the name and re-reserving it from the company is the only alternative, and it opens a race window
on a name that *is* exclusively reserved on Apple's side. So GA-then-transfer is not a preference;
it is the only route that keeps the name, the bundle ID, the installs and the reviews.

**Step 4 after step 2 is free, and it deletes work rather than delaying it.** Nothing has ever
been uploaded to Play, and a package name is claimed by the account that **first uploads** it.
So Android under the company needs no Play app transfer at all, and the 12-tester/14-day closed
test — a rule for *personal* accounts created after 2023-11-13, which ours is — never applies.
The cost of waiting is that v0.1 is one platform; the thing bought is that Android's longest pole
stops existing instead of being rescheduled.

⚠️ **Do not upload anything to Play from the personal account** — not an alpha, not a test. It is
the one irreversible step in the whole sequence, and it is the step that would put the package
name on the wrong account. `android.mjs` stays `blocked` as the interlock.

### What the transfer costs, and why it is affordable *(the part worth knowing before step 3)*

A transfer changes the **signing principal**, and
[`@leapsake/key-custody`](../packages/key-custody/README.md) → *The signing identity owns the
enclave key* is the fact to read first: iOS keychain access groups are prefixed with the Team ID,
so a new team means **every existing enclave key becomes unreadable, for everyone, at once**.

✅ **Confirmed against Apple, not assumed** *(checked 2026-09-06)*. The hopeful reading — that
the App ID keeps its original prefix so keychain access survives — is **wrong**: a transferred
App ID takes the **recipient's** Team ID as its prefix, and since the keychain access group is
`application-identifier` (Team ID + bundle ID), every item in the old group is unreachable to the
transferred app ([TN2311](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/technotes/tn2311/_index.html)).
The standard mitigation — ship a release that copies keychain items into an **app-group**
keychain, wait for adoption, then transfer — is **rejected here**: it buys an entitlement, a
migration release and a wait window in order to save a single password prompt, and app-group
container sharing is itself transfer-restricted for sandboxed Mac apps.

Under *encryption follows custody* that is bounded, and this is the strongest practical
vindication the model has had:

| The user is… | What step 3 costs them |
|---|---|
| **Unauthenticated** (no account) | **nothing** — there are no keys, the store is plaintext, it opens |
| **Authenticated** | **one password entry** at the recovery gate; the phrase only if they have forgotten the password too |

Three consequences, all actionable:

- **Blast radius scales with user count, so keep steps 1→3 close together.** Every day between GA
  and the transfer adds users who will meet that gate. The incorporation running in parallel with
  step 1 is what keeps the gap in days rather than months.
- **The gate needs a sentence, timed to the transfer.** A user who opens the app and is asked for
  a password with no explanation will reasonably conclude they have been hacked. Release notes at
  minimum; a line on the gate itself is better.
- **macOS ships after the transfer for the same reason** (step 5). `safeStorage`'s keychain ACL is
  bound to the code signature, so shipping desktop under the personal Developer ID first would
  pay this cost a second time, on a second platform, for no benefit.

**Step 3 also renames the publisher, in public.** [`../PRIVACY.md`](../PRIVACY.md) says *Joshua
Smith* because that is what the Apple account is *(owner, 2026-08-28)*, and the App Store seller
name has to match it. Incorporating changes both — and the policy is a **live URL a reviewer
reads**, served from [`apps/website`](../apps/website/README.md), so it is a thing the transfer
has to update rather than discover afterwards.

### What must be true before step 3 will start *(Apple's criteria, checked 2026-09-06)*

The transfer is refused, not queued, if any of these is unmet. Two of them reach back into
decisions made *before* the transfer, which is why they are here rather than in a runbook.

- **TestFlight must be fully off** — every build expired, **every tester deleted**, and the Test
  Information fields cleared. This is the one that costs something today: v0.1 has a real external
  tester, and clearing TestFlight ends their access. Tell them to install the App Store build
  **without deleting the TestFlight app first** — same bundle ID, same container, so the update
  keeps their data, while a delete-then-install destroys it and nothing can recover it
  (→ *The backup gap*, below).
- ⚠️ **No version of the app may *ever* have used an iCloud or Passbook entitlement.** Not "does
  not now" — *no version, ever*. This makes it a **permanent, one-way disqualifier**: adding
  iCloud before the transfer — for a backup feature, say — would strand the record on the
  personal account for good. Today `apps/mobile/app.json` is clean (no iCloud, no App Groups, no
  Sign in with Apple, no Apple Pay, no associated domains — only `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`
  and two `LSApplicationQueriesSchemes`), and **it must stay that way until step 3 completes**.
- **At least one version released to the App Store** — step 1, and the reason it is step 1.
- **Not available for pre-order**, both accounts out of any pending or changing state, and the
  latest paid/free agreements accepted on both sides.
- **No in-app purchase product ID may collide** with one in the recipient account. Moot today,
  worth re-reading when the paid relay exists.

⚠️ **Still unconfirmed, and only this**: that Play's tester wall genuinely exempts organization
accounts. Google scopes the rule to personal accounts and simply does not discuss orgs — the
exemption is consistent across secondary sources but not stated by Google in those words.

### The backup gap this sequence walked past — closed by making export a GA blocker *(2026-09-06)*

Not a transfer risk; a v0.1 risk the transfer's TestFlight step happens to aim a user at. There
was **no export and no backup path**: sync is behind `multiDevice: false`, mobile Settings has no
export, and the custody README's own "offer an export first" line had nothing to call. A user's
only copy is the app container. The stores sit under expo-sqlite's default directory so an iCloud
device backup *should* capture them, but that restore has never been tested end to end and the app
promises it nowhere.

➡️ **Resolved by scheduling, not by accepting.** Export moved out of [`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md) and
became the first blocker on [`ios-ga.md`](./ios-ga.md) → 1 — which also carries the constraint
this section is the reason for: **the exporter must not use iCloud**, because an iCloud
entitlement in any shipped version would permanently disqualify the transfer described above. The
obvious design for a backup feature is the one design that cannot be built before step 3.

### Store identity, settled

- ✅ **The App Store Connect record exists** *(owner, 2026-08-19)*, and its bundle ID is
  `com.leapsake.app`, matching `apps/mobile/app.json`. It is chosen at record creation and cannot
  be edited afterwards, so the iOS identity is settled.
- ✅ **The Play-side *name* is not a race** *(checked 2026-08-26)* — correcting an earlier line
  here that said names are first-come-first-served on both stores. That is true of Apple and
  **not** of Play: Play titles are not exclusively reserved, and two apps may share one as long
  as their package names differ, bounded by the impersonation policy rather than by a
  reservation. Nothing needs claiming to protect it.
- ⚠️ **The Play *package name* is the thing that is claimed, and it is claimed by the first
  upload** — globally unique, permanent, and bound to the uploading account. The risk was never
  losing `com.leapsake.app` to a stranger; it is binding it to the wrong account of your own.

**08 and 09 both sat in front of the first store build on purpose** (both now done, ✅ above),
for the same reason from two directions: that is where the first store listing is cut, so
anything that changes what the app *is* (08's two OS permissions) or what it *looks like* (09's
tab bar, in every screenshot) is cheaper before it than after. Landing either afterwards would
have meant a second store-metadata pass mid-flight. The argument was written against Play's
listing and holds unchanged for Apple's.

v0.1        ✅08 ─ ✅09 ─ ✅iOS pipeline ─ ✅beta bar ─ ✅external TestFlight ─ ios-ga.md ─ iOS GA

after v0.1  incorporate + D-U-N-S (starts now, runs in parallel) ───────────────── transfer

## Open decisions for owner sign-off

1. ✅ **How much of the E2E catalog gates v0.1, really?** — **settled as drafted**
   *(owner, 2026-08-28)*. **The rule's unit is *platform × rung*, not platform**: the full
   catalog is still required before Leapsake is a product anyone can buy into, but the earliest
   rung a stranger installs does not carry the whole of it on day one. The rule and its rung
   table are permanent and live in [`../CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md) → *The E2E release
   gate*; the per-flow column is [`testing/crucial-flows.md`](./testing/crucial-flows.md) →
   *the matrix*.

   **The owner's reasoning, because it decides more than this question does:** *alpha and
   beta can be buggy — aim high, but bugs are acceptable. What must be avoided is potential
   **data loss** once v0.1 is stable, and it is unacceptable at v1.0. Right now the important
   thing is to get this in front of testers, on real devices, and learn how people use it.*

   That is a **ratchet whose axis is data loss rather than defect count**, which is why it
   settles this cleanly: the deferred flows (7b/7c) are exactly the ones that prove data can be
   *given back*, and the rung table already demands them before `rc` — one rung before anyone
   keeps real records here. **The bar rises with the rung; it never falls.**

   **The line it draws:** *beta proves the app does not lose data in normal use; rc proves it
   can give data back when the OS loses the key.*

   **The risk accepted, stated plainly:** at beta, nothing automated proves the recovery doors
   open, so a tester whose iOS keychain is reset (a device restore does this) could be stranded
   with an unreadable store. The mitigation is one sentence in
   [`release-notes/what-to-test.txt`](../release-notes/what-to-test.txt) telling testers the
   build is not a sole copy of anything — which is why that file is a **preflight requirement
   rather than decoration**, and `rc` is where it stops being enough. **That trade was weighed
   and accepted** *(owner, 2026-08-28)*. The alternative considered and declined was promoting
   the doors into the beta bar: 7c was cheap enough to be tempting — `dev-clear-dbkey` and the
   `RecoveryGate` both already exist, so it needs three `testID`s and a flow — and the answer
   was still that a build in testers' hands now beats proving a door a beta tester was told not
   to depend on. It is `rc`'s, and `rc` is inside v0.1.

   ⚠️ **Two things this did not trade, and still does not.** `--strict` stays strict and the
   `e2e` tier must be genuinely `ready` and **green** — never blocked-tolerated, never exempted
   the way `alpha` is; the grading changes what the gate *contains*, not whether it runs.
   And **7b carries a cost this decision's draft got wrong**: Flow 4 as built does not capture
   the recovery phrase — it asserts the grid has a 24th word and no 25th — so 7b needs 24
   stitched `copyTextFrom` calls or a new surface exposing the phrase as one string. 7c escapes
   it entirely, being answered with the password Flow 4 already typed.

2. **Flows 6/7a in the v0.1 gate or fast-follow?** (1) is now settled, and it points this the
   same way: both flows need two instances, and neither is on the data-loss ratchet for a
   build that ships with sync switched off. **Flow 6 largely
   answers itself**: *enable sync and pair a second device* exercises relay sync, and v0.1
   ships with `multiDevice` **off** in [`@leapsake/flags`](../packages/flags/README.md) — every
   door to it is shut. A v0.1 user cannot reach that flow, so gating the release on it would
   mean gating on a path the build does not expose, provable only by flipping a flag that
   production does not set. It belongs to the release that turns sync **on**, and until then
   the on-state's coverage is that README's obligation rather than this gate's. Flow **7a**
   (cross-device recovery) is a different case: the feature *is* reachable in v0.1, it is
   simply expensive to automate — two instances — and what it proves overlaps decision 5
   below. Decide 7a with 5, not with 6.
3. ✅ **Retired 2026-08-26** — *auto-update before or after going public* was a desktop
   question, and desktop left v0.1. It returns with
   [`desktop-packaging.md`](./desktop-packaging.md) → C.
4. ✅ **Which Play account publishes v0.1 — and therefore whether the tester wall applies at
   all?** — **settled 2026-09-06** *(owner)*: **neither, for v0.1.** Android leaves the release
   rather than choosing an account, and the company account publishes it later.

   The question assumed the choice had to be made *before* v0.1 because Android was in v0.1.
   Taking Android out dissolves it: nothing is published to Play from anywhere, so the personal
   account never claims the package name, no Play app transfer is ever needed, and the
   12-tester/14-day wall — a rule for personal accounts, which the company's is not — never
   binds. **What was the critical path became a non-event.**

   The full ordering, what it costs at the Apple end, and the two claims still worth verifying
   are in *The account sequence* above. [`android-pipeline.md`](./android-pipeline.md) carries
   the Android-specific half, and the `blocked` interlock that makes the one irreversible step
   impossible to take by accident.

5. **Does v0.1 ship without merge by recovery phrase?** Deferred while building the merge
   *(owner, 2026-08-08)*. `recoverAccount` refuses a device that already holds an account exactly
   as `joinAccount` does, so merging by phrase needs the same copy-first treatment and is a second
   full flow on both clients. The gap: a user who has their account's **phrase** but not its
   password must recover on their *other* device first — which is fine unless that device is the
   one they lost. **Leaning ship-without** (password-plus-a-second-device covers the realistic
   case, and the flow it would duplicate is the most delicate one we have), but it is a real hole
   in the "cannot strand anyone" promise, so it is your call. Context:
   [`encryption/model.md`](./encryption/model.md) §7.2.2.

## Explicitly not v0.1

Everything else lives in [`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md) — including work that is *ready* and merely
non-blocking (vCard export, reminder search, dedup quality, the onboarding import nudge). The
rule is strict on purpose: **if it does not gate v0.1, it waits.** That is what keeps this list
short enough to finish.

**08 and 09 are the exceptions, and both are written down as ones** so neither can be cited as
precedent.

**08** because a reminders app with no way to reach a user outside the app is not shippable, and
email — the obvious alternative channel — is itself out of scope for v0.1. Notification *privacy
levels* and the *desktop* applier both stayed behind the rule and remain deferred to
[`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md).

**09** because the tab bar is in every store screenshot and the first ones are cut for the first
store listing — Play's when 09 ran, Apple's now. Note what it
did *not* do, which is what keeps it an exception rather than a crack: it changed navigation and
surface colour and stopped there. Typography, an icon set, and the illustrated empty states are
untouched and remain [`v0-2.md`](./v0-2.md)'s *styling / the design system* — a screenshot is
cheap to re-cut for a nicer typeface, and expensive to re-cut for a different set of tabs.

**Multi-device sync is built and held back** *(owner, 2026-08-21)*, not unbuilt. The relay, the
join, the merge, and the scheduler all work; v0.1 simply does not show them, behind
`multiDevice` in [`@leapsake/flags`](../packages/flags/README.md). This is the one entry here
that is a *switch* rather than a scope line, so it carries a switch's obligations: the gates are
listed in that README, the on-state stays under test, and the removal is a v0.2 item rather than
a thing to remember. Turning it back on for a session is
`LEAPSAKE_FLAGS=multiDevice pnpm desktop`.

**Out of scope entirely for v0.1:** Windows and Linux desktop, matching the E2E host policy —
blocked, not waived. Windows code-signing certs carry their own procurement latency and, for
OV/EV, hardware-token logistics.
